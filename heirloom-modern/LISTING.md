## How it's dated

The extension runs on galvanised steel slides stamped **"Watertown Table Slide Corp. ·
Patent Pending · Watertown, Wisconsin."** Watertown invented the wooden table slide in 1886
and supplied the best American furniture houses — but they did not introduce a *galvanised
steel* slide until **1957**. So this table was built in 1957 or after, most likely within a
few years of it.

Under the apron, burned into the wood rather than printed, is the factory number **30**.

That's not a dealer's estimate. It's stamped into the hardware, and you can read it yourself
with a flashlight.

## About the maker

George Widdicomb trained as a cabinetmaker in England, emigrated in 1845, and opened a shop
in Grand Rapids in 1857 with his four sons. All four enlisted in the Civil War and the shop
failed in 1864 — then the brothers came home and started again. The firm incorporated as the
Widdicomb Furniture Company in 1873, and in **1897 John Widdicomb left to found his own
company**, the maker of this table.

From 1898 its furniture was drawn by one man: **Ralph H. Widdicomb**, who took first prize at
the **1900 Paris Exposition** and stayed at the drawing board for fifty-three years until
1951. This table sits just past the end of his run, at the handoff to J. Stuart Clingman,
whose sculpted walnut dining furniture defines the company's mid-century look.

The name retired in 2002 after 145 years. Nothing further was made.
