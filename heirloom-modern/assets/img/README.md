# Photo drop-in guide

Save your photos into this folder using **exactly these filenames**. Any file that
isn't here yet shows a labelled placeholder on the page instead of a broken image,
so the site never looks broken while you're still shooting.

| Filename | What it is | How to shoot it |
|---|---|---|
| `hero.jpg` | Full-bleed background behind the headline | Wide, fairly dark, lots of empty space on the left for text to sit over. Landscape, 2400px wide+ |
| `full-extended.jpg` | **The money shot** | All 3 leaves in, low corner angle, chairs pulled out |
| `closed.jpg` | Table closed | Same angle as the extended shot — makes a great before/after pair |
| `top-down.jpg` | The book-matched top | Straight down from a step-ladder, side window light, no flash |
| `detail-grain.jpg` | Grain close-up | Close on the centre seam where the mirror-match shows |
| `detail-pedestal.jpg` | Pedestal | Low three-quarter angle so the sculpted form reads |
| `detail-edge.jpg` | Edge profile | Eye level, raking light down the long edge |
| `leaves-stacked.jpg` | The three leaves | All three stood or stacked together |
| `makers-mark.jpg` | The stamp underneath | Buyers look for this — get it sharp and well lit |
| `room-setting.jpg` | In the room | Wide, chairs in, natural light |
| `archival-factory.jpg` | *Optional* period Grand Rapids image | Only use one you actually hold rights to |

## Shooting notes

- **Daylight, no flash.** Walnut figure dies under direct flash. Shoot near a window
  on an overcast day, or in the hour before sunset.
- **Wipe it down first** and shoot from a low angle — chest height or below makes a
  table look substantial.
- **Clear the room.** Empty background, nothing on the top.
- Resize to about **2000–2400px on the long edge** and save as JPEG quality ~80.
  Anything larger just slows the page down.

## Before you publish

Only use photographs you took or otherwise own. Product photos from another dealer's
listing belong to that dealer — reusing them on your own sales page is a copyright
problem, and your own photos of your own table will sell it better anyway.
